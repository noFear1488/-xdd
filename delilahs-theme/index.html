About this piece

Delilah Copperspoon's music in Dishonored 2 is Daniel Licht's, and it is under copyright — so this is not a transcription of it. It is an original arrangement written in that idiom: the things his Delilah cues actually do, done here with new material.

Chiefly that means D harmonic minor — the raised seventh (C♯) leaning on the tonic gives the lullaby its sour, witch-tale edge — carried by a music box over sustained low strings, in a waltz slow enough that the meter reads as menace rather than dance.

How it is built

Four bars of open fifths in the Void, then A — the theme, low and close. B opens onto B♭ and climbs. A′ returns an octave up, marked 8va, where the box turns brittle and glassy. Four bars of coda fall back to a held D minor.

The rhythmic signature is the dotted eighth and sixteenth on beat two — the small stumble in the waltz, the step that isn't quite even.

What you're looking at

The staves, beams, ledger lines and 8va bracket are engraved to SVG at load from the note data — no notation library. The same data drives a Web Audio engine: the box is additive with slightly inharmonic partials and a fast decay, the strings are detuned saws under a moving low-pass, both fed to a comb-filter reverb.

In the Void panel, each note throws a shaft of light — horizontal position is pitch, colour is voice.
